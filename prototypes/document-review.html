Trailhead briefing

This is a flat-fee freelance web development agreement. It lets Harlow Digital Studio end the engagement at any time without paying for unaccepted work, claims your pre-existing tools and frameworks (not just what you build for them), holds you responsible for their legal costs with no cap, and restricts you from similar work for competitors anywhere in the country for five years after the engagement ends.

Freelance Web Development Agreement

Between Harlow Digital Studio ("Client") and the undersigned independent contractor ("Contractor")

1.Scope of Work. Contractor will design, build, and deliver a responsive marketing website of up to twelve (12) pages, including all source files, on the timeline set out in Exhibit A.

2.Term and Termination. This Agreement begins on the Effective Date and continues until the Deliverables are accepted. Client may terminate this Agreement at any time, for any reason or no reason, upon written notice to Contractor, and Contractor shall have no right to cure and no entitlement to payment for Deliverables not yet formally accepted as of the termination date.

Why flagged: CriticalClient can end the engagement at will with no cure period, and you forfeit payment for anything not yet "formally accepted." Work you already delivered can go unpaid entirely at Client's discretion.
Counter-offer"Either party may terminate this Agreement upon fifteen (15) days' written notice. If Client terminates, Contractor shall be paid in full for all Deliverables completed or substantially completed as of the termination date, whether or not formally accepted."

3.Fees. Client shall pay Contractor a flat fee of $8,400, payable in three installments. Any Change Order requested after the initial scope is fixed shall be billed at a rate set by Client in its sole discretion at the time of the request.

Why flagged: ModerateClient, not you, sets the rate for any work beyond the original scope: a recurring exposure every time the project grows.
Counter-offer"Change Orders shall be billed at Contractor's standard hourly rate of $125/hour, disclosed in Exhibit A, and require Contractor's written approval before work begins."

4.Intellectual Property. Contractor hereby assigns to Client, in perpetuity and throughout the universe, all right, title, and interest in and to any and all pre-existing tools, frameworks, code libraries, and design systems that Contractor uses, modifies, or incorporates in connection with the Deliverables.

Why flagged: CriticalThis reaches beyond the site you're building them: it claims the reusable tools and frameworks you bring to every client. That's closer to losing a business asset than losing one deliverable.
Counter-offer"Contractor assigns to Client all right, title, and interest in the Deliverables created specifically for Client under this Agreement. Contractor's pre-existing tools, frameworks, code libraries, and design systems remain Contractor's property and are licensed to Client for use with the Deliverables."

5.Non-Competition. During the Term and for a period of five (5) years following termination of this Agreement, Contractor shall not, directly or indirectly, provide website design or development services to any business offering products or services similar to those of Client, anywhere in the United States.

Why flagged: CriticalFive years, nationwide, against any similar business: this restricts a meaningful share of your future paid work. Foregone income counts as financial exposure even though no money changes hands today.
Counter-offer"For a period of six (6) months following termination, Contractor shall not knowingly perform substantially similar website design services for a direct competitor of Client that Client identifies in writing at signing."
Enforceability of non-compete clauses varies significantly by state. Verify locally before relying on this restriction, or on this counter-offer, as legally binding.

6.Indemnification. Contractor shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless Client from and against any and all claims, damages, losses, and expenses, including attorneys' fees, arising out of or relating to Contractor's performance of this Agreement, without regard to the amount of such claims, damages, losses, or expenses.

Why flagged: CriticalNo cap. You could end up covering Client's legal costs in full, regardless of size, for anything connected to your work: the exact exposure that has left freelancers self-funding a client's legal defense.
Counter-offer"Contractor's indemnification obligations under this Section are limited to claims arising from Contractor's gross negligence or willful misconduct, and Contractor's total liability under this Agreement shall not exceed the total fees paid under this Agreement."

7.Confidentiality. Each party agrees to keep confidential any proprietary information disclosed by the other party during the Term.

8.Governing Law. This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of the State of Delaware.

9.Assignment. Client may assign this Agreement, in whole or in part, to any successor, acquirer, or affiliate without Contractor's prior consent.

Why flagged: Worth notingYou could end up working for, and owed by, a company you never agreed to. Low exposure on its own, but worth knowing before you sign.
Counter-offer"Client may not assign this Agreement without Contractor's prior written consent, which shall not be unreasonably withheld."

Severity legend

  • Critical: direct or foregone-income exposure
  • Moderate: real, lesser exposure
  • Worth noting: one-sided, not dangerous
