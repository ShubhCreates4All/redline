# Existing Contract-Review / Clause-Analysis Products — Market Scan

Research date: 2026-08-28. Compiled via web search and direct review-site fetches (search engine + G2/Trustpilot/Chrome Web Store). Scope limited per research guardrails (max 12 searches, max 15 page reads, stop at 8 well-sourced findings — 9 were reached).

---

### LawDepot
**What it does:** Online legal document generator — template-based contracts, leases, wills, NDAs, etc. Users answer a questionnaire and it assembles a document; not AI clause-risk analysis, more a form-filling service (some AI features referenced in newer marketing but core product is templates).
**Target user:** Consumers and small businesses needing a one-off legal document without a lawyer.
**Pricing:** One-Year Pro subscription ~$107.88–$155.88/yr (~$8.99–$12.99/mo billed annually); one-time "Basic License" for a single document ranges $7.50–$120 depending on document type; free 7-day trial that auto-converts to a paid plan if not cancelled.
**Most common complaint:** Billing/subscription transparency — the free trial silently converts to a recurring paid subscription. Trustpilot reviews describe it as "Simple" but "very deceiving on up front conditions," with a review summary noting "monthly pricing plans were not clearly indicated during initial signup, leading to frustrating surprise costs." (Source: Trustpilot)
**Source(s):** https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/lawdepot.com ; https://www.g2.com/products/lawdepot/reviews

### Rocket Lawyer (incl. "Rocket Copilot Contract Review")
**What it does:** Legal document platform combining templates, e-signature, and attorney consultations; recently added "Rocket Copilot Contract Review," an AI feature for evaluating/flagging issues in contracts, built on the company's proprietary legal-analysis data.
**Target user:** Consumers, freelancers, and small businesses wanting DIY legal documents plus optional attorney access.
**Pricing:** Free trial requires a credit card and auto-converts to $39.99/month; annual plan ~$239.88/yr under the "Rocket Legal+" membership tier (unlimited documents, AI contract review, attorney consults).
**Most common complaint:** Billing practices — the free trial's automatic conversion to $39.99/month is cited repeatedly in BBB/review complaints, along with difficulty getting refunds beyond one month. Trustpilot review summary: "Some reviewers were not happy with unexpected subscription charges, annual membership renewals, or confusing cancellation processes," despite an overall "Excellent" 4.5/5 rating. Separately, users note the AI/templates can miss "essential legal clauses" in complex, non-standard scenarios.
**Source(s):** https://www.trustpilot.com/review/rocketlawyer.com ; https://www.rocketlawyer.com/newsroom/rocket-lawyer-launches-rocket-copilot-contract-review ; https://www.g2.com/products/rocket-lawyer-rocket-lawyer/reviews

### DoNotPay
**What it does:** "Robot lawyer" consumer app — automates disputes, cancels subscriptions, generates demand letters/small-claims filings, and offers a general AI legal-chat add-on; marketed broadly rather than as a dedicated contract-review tool.
**Target user:** Individual consumers looking for low-cost/free self-help legal actions.
**Pricing:** ~$36 charged roughly every two months (effectively a recurring subscription) plus a $10/month AI-chat add-on; pricing/terms are widely reported as unclear at signup.
**Most common complaint:** Deceptive billing and near-impossible cancellation. Trustpilot rating is 1.8/5 from 379 reviews. Representative quotes pulled directly from reviews: "Without my consent they charged my bank account $36 for a 'subscription' that I NEVER signed up for," and "Makes it literally impossible to cancel your subscription, and continues to bill you even after they themselves have confirmed cancellation." The company is also under an FTC consent order (Feb 2025) and a California class action (Price v. DoNotPay Inc.) alleging unauthorized practice of law.
**Source(s):** https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/donotpay.com?page=8 ; https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/DoNotPayInc-Complaint.pdf

### Ironclad (CLM)
**What it does:** Enterprise contract lifecycle management (CLM) platform — workflow/approval automation, a contract repository, redlining/collaboration, and AI-assisted review/metadata extraction across the contract lifecycle.
**Target user:** Mid-market to enterprise legal, sales, and procurement teams managing high contract volume.
**Pricing:** Custom/quote-based; reviewer estimates put entry pricing around $500+/month, billed per user, with additional implementation fees and charges for template changes — pricing is not publicly listed.
**Most common complaint:** Search/findability inside the contract repository — described as "finicky if you don't know the exact metadata or tags used," turning contract lookup into "a scavenger hunt" when documents weren't tagged well on upload. Also frequently cited: a steep learning curve and high cost for smaller businesses. Overall ratings remain strong (4.5/5 G2, 4.4/5 Capterra).
**Source(s):** https://www.g2.com/products/ironclad/reviews ; https://www.hyperstart.com/blog/ironclad-reviews/

### Juro
**What it does:** AI-native contract collaboration/CLM platform — browser-native contract editor, workflow automation, and an AI assistant for extracting data, summarizing, and negotiating contracts (positioned as more lightweight/modern than legacy CLM).
**Target user:** Mid-market business teams (roughly 100–1,000 contracts/year) — legal, sales, and HR — rather than large enterprise or individual consumers.
**Pricing:** Not publicly published; requires a sales quote. Third-party estimates cite roughly $1,000–$5,000/month depending on scale.
**Most common complaint:** Opaque, custom pricing that's hard to evaluate upfront and is described by reviewers as "steep for smaller organizations"; some also flag limited integrations and difficulty with complex contract formatting. Ratings are otherwise strong (4.6/5 G2, 4.8/5 Capterra).
**Source(s):** https://www.g2.com/products/juro/reviews ; https://aavenir.com/juro-pricing/

### Spellbook
**What it does:** AI contracting copilot that lives inside Microsoft Word — runs first-pass reviews of contracts, suggests redlines, drafts clauses grounded in the user's own playbook/positions, and flags deviations from market-standard or firm-preferred language.
**Target user:** Transactional/commercial lawyers and in-house legal teams doing contract drafting and negotiation (not a consumer product).
**Pricing:** Not publicly fixed — custom quotes based on team size and usage; one third-party source lists informal tiers around $99/user/month (Starter) and $149/user/month (Professional), but this is not confirmed on Spellbook's own site.
**Most common complaint:** AI reliability — reviewers note it "is prone to making mistakes, so you have to be vigilant to ensure that they are caught," including occasionally inserting wrong citations; formatting consistency in generated output is also flagged as inconsistent.
**Source(s):** https://www.g2.com/products/spellbook/reviews ; https://www.hyperstart.com/blog/spellbook-pricing/

### Robin AI
**What it does:** AI legal-review platform for businesses — analyzes contracts against a company's playbook/risk positions, surfaces key clauses and risk flags, and assists with negotiation to speed up review cycles.
**Target user:** Mid-size to large companies' legal/commercial teams (positioned as an alternative to outside counsel for routine contract review), not individual consumers.
**Pricing:** No public self-serve pricing; third-party estimates vary widely — some sources cite plans starting near $5,000/year, others cite enterprise deployments in the $30,000–$80,000/year range, or custom pricing around $300+/user/month. Could not confirm an authoritative figure directly from Robin AI's own site.
**Most common complaint:** Could not find a specific, well-sourced recurring complaint — review coverage found was largely promotional/positive ("ease of use," "time-saving"), with the main caveat being that "nuanced legal interpretation still requires human verification" rather than a concrete user gripe. Flagging pricing opacity as the closest thing to a consistent friction point.
**Source(s):** https://www.g2.com/products/robin-2025-07-08/reviews

### Lexion (now part of DocuSign)
**What it does:** AI-powered contract management — automatic metadata/clause extraction, contract repository, renewal tracking, and workflow tools; acquired by DocuSign, now being positioned within DocuSign's Intelligent Agreement Management (IAM) suite.
**Target user:** Mid-market to enterprise legal/ops teams managing a contract repository at scale.
**Pricing:** Not publicly listed; G2 reviewers flag it as expensive, especially for smaller organizations; post-acquisition pricing is expected to fold into DocuSign's broader commercial framework.
**Most common complaint:** Cost — "expensive," particularly for smaller organizations — followed by AI accuracy limitations ("common limitation with the AI accuracy...improvements are needed in its data interpretation") and limited/inflexible customization options. Overall rating is strong (4.6/5, 134+ G2 reviews).
**Source(s):** https://www.g2.com/products/lexion/reviews ; https://www.g2.com/products/lexion/reviews?qs=pros-and-cons

### ToS;DR (Terms of Service; Didn't Read)
**What it does:** Free, community/volunteer-curated browser extension (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari, Opera) that grades a website's Terms of Service and Privacy Policy from A (best) to E (worst) and shows short plain-language summaries of good/neutral/bad clauses when you visit a compatible site. Not AI-native — relies on human curators reviewing and scoring documents.
**Target user:** General consumers/privacy-conscious internet users, not businesses or contract professionals.
**Pricing:** Free (volunteer/nonprofit project, no paid tier found).
**Most common complaint:** Coverage and permissions concerns — the extension only works on sites already reviewed by volunteers (many sites are simply not covered), and at least one reviewer flagged discomfort with the browsing-history access the extension needs to function: "great app. not sure why you got to comb through my browser history like that though :(". Other user reports (from broader search) mention alerts feeling "anxiety-inducing" on low-scoring sites and occasional performance/slowness complaints.
**Source(s):** https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/terms-of-service-didn%E2%80%99t-r/hjdoplcnndgiblooccencgcggcoihigg/reviews ; https://tosdr.org/en
